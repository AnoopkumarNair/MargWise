# Pricing and market notes (arithmetic, not forecasts)

All inputs are assumptions to be replaced by pilot data: price includes 18% GST, payment fees about 2%, support and refunds about Rs 4 per paying user, fixed annual cost Rs 25 lakh (the review's example), delivery cost per school Rs 14,000 (the review's example).

## Direct to parent or student at Rs 99

- Net of GST: Rs 83.9. After payment fees and support: about Rs 77 contribution per paying user.
- Break-even on Rs 25 lakh fixed cost: about 32,500 paying users a year, with zero acquisition cost.
- At a 2% free-to-paid conversion (an assumption; free tools often convert lower), that needs about 1.6 million free users a year.
- If a paying user costs Rs 150 to 500 to acquire through ads (typical range for Indian consumer edtech, not measured here), each sale loses Rs 70 to 420.
- Free alternatives (CBSE's career dashboard and My Career Advisor, free assessments) cap what parents will pay for content alone.

Conclusion: Rs 99 direct only works if almost all users arrive for free and convert at a high rate. Do not plan on it.

## Through schools at about Rs 99 per student per year

- 500 students at Rs 99 is Rs 49,500 a school before GST. With Rs 14,000 delivery cost, contribution is about Rs 35,500 (72%).
- Break-even on Rs 25 lakh fixed cost: about 70 schools (about 35,000 students).
- With 200 students a school: Rs 19,800 revenue and about Rs 5,800 contribution. Use a flat floor of Rs 25,000 to 40,000 a school, or Rs 149 to 199 a student, or cut delivery cost below Rs 5,000 through automation.
- The cost that decides this is human sales and support time, not hosting.

## Where the edge is, honestly

Copyable in weeks by a funded competitor: the quiz and game, the career list, public-data layers (O*NET, BLS, PLFS, AI exposure).
Harder to copy: paid school relationships and renewals, a trusted independent position with no placement or college referral income, consented outcome data, a maintained India pathway dataset, and a very low cost to operate and refresh.
None of these exists yet at scale. Treat the business case as unproven until at least two schools pay for a pilot.
