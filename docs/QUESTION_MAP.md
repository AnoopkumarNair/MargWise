# Question map: what each card measures and how it feeds the result

_Generated by `engine/tools/question_map.mjs` from the shipped engine (journey-v2-draft) and `data/story2.json`. Do not edit by hand; run the tool._

## 1. Constructs
The game measures **interest**, not ability. It uses the six Holland (RIASEC) types, because the career data it matches against (O*NET) is expressed in the same six types.

| Type | In the game | What it means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| R Realistic | Builder | working with tools, machines, the outdoors; practical, hands-on problem solving |
| I Investigative | Thinker | working things out, observing, analysing, asking why |
| A Artistic | Creator | making and expressing, imagining, designing, open-ended work |
| S Social | Helper | teaching, caring, guiding, working through people |
| E Enterprising | Persuader | leading, organising others, selling an idea, taking initiative and risk |
| C Conventional | Organiser | order, accuracy, systems, records, following clear procedures |

## 2. How an answer becomes a result
1. **Each stop shows four cards and the student spends two flasks.** Every card expresses one type as its main load (1.0) and a related type as a smaller load (0.5), for example "take the engine apart" is Realistic 1.0 plus Investigative 0.5. Choosing a card says "I would enjoy this more than the other cards here".
2. **Forced choice.** Because the four cards in a stop are four different types, the student cannot say yes to everything. The estimate is relative (what stands out for this student), which is why the app calls it an interest shape and not a score.
3. **Estimate.** The choices are combined by a multinomial-logit model into six trait estimates with an uncertainty. Stops are chosen adaptively. After 8 stops a draft result is ready and the student may stop there or keep going (up to 15 stops) to sharpen it; the walk ends early once the leading traits are clear. No stop is ever asked twice.
4. **Match to careers.** The six-trait shape is correlated with each career's O*NET interest profile; a field's score is the mean of its best four careers, converted to a percentile against a reference set and balanced across fields (`data/fieldbias.json`).
5. **Not used for ranking:** the lamp-lock puzzles show rough aptitude signals on the results page but do not change the field order until they are validated.

**How the two flasks work.**
- **Both flasks on one card** counts that card twice: a stronger signal that you prefer it to the other three.
- **One flask on each of two cards** counts each once: a signal for both, weaker for either.
- **Keeping your oil** counts as "none of these" and adds almost nothing to the estimate (a student who keeps most of their oil gets a "not enough signal" note).
- Neither way of spending is more correct. In a test case, putting both flasks on a Builder card raised the Builder estimate by about 0.1 on the model's scale, and splitting between a Builder and a Creator card raised Creator by about 0.15 and left Builder where it was. One stop is one of 8 to 15, so no single spend decides the result.

## 5. Multiple signals, not one memorable scene
A journey uses 8 to 15 of the 15 stops (two flasks each): 8 for the draft, more if the student sharpens it. A leave-one-out test (`engine/tests/design.test.mjs`) removes each stop in turn from 60 simulated journeys and checks how often the top field changes; the test fails if any single stop changes the top field in more than 35 percent of journeys (a draft has the fewest stops, so it is the most sensitive). The current worst case is shown in the test output. No single stop decides the result, and the top three areas always rest on several independent choices.

## 6. Known limits
- The mapping of cards to types is a design judgement, not yet validated against a published interest inventory or against real students (see docs/CALIBRATION.md).
- Card wording is audited for social desirability (see docs/CARD_AUDIT.md), but one audit is not a validation; real data on how often each card is picked is needed.
- Seeded shuffling removes position effects between students, not within one student.

| Check | Who | How |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Do the cards read as the activity they are meant to express, and are they understood by a Class 9 to 12 student? | A school counsellor or teacher | Read the cards above; in the pilot, a think-aloud session with a few students |
| Does the result look sensible for this student? | The school counsellor | Locks their own top 3 before the student plays, then compares (the counsellor review page) |
| Are the facts on each field page right? | Two fact-check reviewers | docs/REVIEW_PROCESS.md |
| Are the questions reliable and valid (do they measure what they claim, consistently, fairly across groups)? | A psychometrician, using pilot data | Item pick rates, test-retest, comparison with an established interest inventory, fairness checks by gender, board and region. A counsellor's agreement is useful evidence but is not psychometric validation |
