# Playtest report: Dev (port 9107, laptop 1366x768)

## 1. Persona
Dev, 14, Class 9, ICSE, Mumbai. Autistic, very literal reader. Loves Indian Railways timetables, small Python scripts, maps. Reads about 180 wpm, loves logic and pattern puzzles, dislikes metaphors, vague words and unclear rules.

## 2. Did he finish? Quit point
He finished (the logic puzzles and the "show me how it was calculated" page hold him). He would NOT close the tab, but he came close twice. Total real time estimate: about 33 to 36 minutes (the cover says "About 15 minutes"). This is my estimate from word counts at 180 wpm plus thinking time; not driver time.
- WOULD QUIT HERE (soft, "he would stop and ask a parent"): screen "Second look" (Herbalists' Garden again), after 6 towns and 2 lock stages (4 puzzles), about minute 17. Reason, in his words: "I already did this garden. Why is it the same four choices again? Is it checking whether I lie? Nobody said there is a check. The counter also went UP from '1 to 8 more' to '2 to 9 more'." He continued only because puzzles were coming.
- Second weak point: the 20 minute mark (towns 9 to 11), when the 4-choice format has repeated 10 times and nothing new happens. Continued because the counter said "up to 4 more".
- Hard quit risk BEFORE the game: the intro and consent screens (about 650 words, about 4 min) include a long privacy text and a consent check. He reads it all, carefully.

## 4. Top 5 moments that lost him
1. "You have two flasks of oil. Spend them on the two things you would most enjoy: both on one if you love it, or one on each... How you light the lamps will show whose road this is." Mechanic is explained as a story. He cannot tell what a flask does to the result, what "keep my oil" gives, or how the "whose road" claim is computed. Also the button text "Light the lamp · keep my oil" has two meanings.
2. Second look: "The lamp at Herbalists' Garden is flickering, as if it wants a second look." Repeats a town he has done, no reason, and the progress label changes from "Second look · 1 to 8 more" to "Second look · 2 to 9 more" after answering. Ends with "The flame holds. Thank you for looking again." which tells him nothing.
3. Consent: "Is a parent or guardian with you? ... Students under 18 should not do it alone." The only buttons are "Yes, a parent has agreed or is with me" and "Go back". If the parent is not there, he is forced to click a statement that is not true, or leave. Same earlier: "I am 18 or older, or my parent or guardian knows about this and agrees."
4. Towns where no option fits him, e.g. River Camp ("Care for the tired travellers... / Dream up a new boat shape... / Run the camp meeting... / Build a raft...") and Herbalists' Garden. The "keep my oil" escape is not explained, and its "Tap again to keep your oil and light" confirmation resets after a few seconds.
5. Progress counter: "Place 1 of 15 · 7 to 14 more" (and "5 to 12 more", "up to 7 more"). A literal reader sees 1 of 15 yet "7 to 14 more" and cannot compute it; it also changes after the Second look. Related: "All the lamps are lit. Let the festival begin!" at place 6 of 15, and "15 lamps are burning behind you" after 15 plus a second look.

## 5. Top 3 moments that worked
1. The shape locks ("Which one is the same shape, just turned around? A flipped copy does not count.") and the number-pattern locks (2, 4, 3, 6, 5, 10, 9, ?). Clear rule, one answer, he can verify it himself. The "flipped copy does not count" sentence is exactly the kind of rule he needs.
2. Towns that happen to match his real interests: Clockmaker's Lane ("Study the pattern of missed chimes", "Write up a step-by-step repair checklist"), Festival Square ("Keep the timetable so every act starts on time"), Fog Pass ("Map where the fog is thickest"). He read them fast and picked in seconds.
3. The result "See which choices pointed here" section: "Observatory Hill: you picked 'Chart the stars and wonder what is out there' (Thinker)." plus "This is about what you enjoy, not what you are good at." This is the exact trace he wanted. Result card "Technology and data" fits (he codes).

## 6. Story
Did not care much. Ila is a flat helper, the lamplighter's clues always sound like a description of the choice type, not a mystery with something to deduce. "A pattern is forming" and "Every keeper remembers the lamplighter a little differently" make him look for a pattern that is never shown. The mystery is never answered before the result (the finale line "The road was never mine. Choose yours." is a metaphor). A character called "Dev, the clockmaker's apprentice" appeared; he thought it was him and was confused. What would make him care: a real puzzle plot (a map or timetable he can reason over, clues that narrow suspects), e.g. the lamplighter's route drawn as a railway-style line map where each town is a station and each clue fills a gap. Also he would like the 15 towns shown as a route diagram with progress.

## 7. Questions
- 15 town screens + 1 second look + 8 lock puzzles (4 stages of 2) + class/board/stream (3 questions) + a 5-point self-rating. Too many: the towns. 15 screens with the same 4-option format; after about 9 he picks by reflex.
- Repetitive: the 4-option town format; second look; locks that repeat the sentence "The old lamplighter sealed this gate. Jyo says any key you try teaches the lantern a little about you." (8 times, unexplained: "a little about you" contradicts "Nothing is marked").
- Confusing (quotes): "Spend them on the two things you would most enjoy: both on one if you love it, or one on each. Pick what you really like, not a bit of everything." (what if I like only one thing a bit; why not everything?); "Where is your mind right now?"; "Light the lamp · keep my oil"; "Map is to traveller as recipe is to ..." (cook or meal both defensible); each option with a negative clause such as "though the answer may come too late", "with oily hands and no one to ask for help", "though it may turn out to be junk" (he cannot tell if the clause lowers the value of the choice).

## 8. Lock puzzles
Fun for him, not exam-like: shapes, patterns, LCM, flask maths. Problems: no feedback on right or wrong ("Click. The lock turns and the gate swings open." appears for every answer), so a logic lover cannot learn; the claim "nothing is marked" next to "teaches the lantern a little about you" is ambiguous; the result says "Strong" for all four puzzle areas, so he does not know what threshold or score that means. "Pick a key" and "Skip this lock" are fine.

## 9. Result
Fit: yes in the top field (Technology and data, Thinker + Organiser), partly: no trace of maps, railways or transport/geography as a field, Creator in top 3 is from just two picks and the result admits ties ("the order of the top three is not fixed"). He understood "Thinker" and "Organiser". Not understood: the radar chart (no numbers, white dots vs coloured numbers), "Further from the middle means you enjoyed it more, compared with your own other choices", "Fairly clear / Why?", "Close to the next one". Pay and job growth are in US dollars and US data. Would he show a parent: probably yes, the "Summary for parents" button; he would show the "choices that pointed here" list. I did NOT open the "How we got this result" link; he would, so that is untested (my guess: it is what he wants).

## 10. Five suggestions, ranked
1. Replace the "flask" story rule with a literal rule on the town screen: "Pick 2 items. Tap the same item twice if you like it twice. Each pick adds 1 point to that interest. You will see all points at the end." State what "keep my oil" does (0 points, nothing is lost) or remove that button's second meaning.
2. Remove or explain the "Second look": say "You answered this town before. We ask again to check that your answers are steady. Your first answers are kept." Never change the progress counter upwards; show "Place 8 of 15" or "7 places left", one exact number.
3. Add an "I don't like any of these" option on every town and a visible "none fit" note, and let a not-there parent be handled honestly (e.g. "My parent does not know yet" with a message on what happens next) so he is not forced to click a statement that is not true.
4. Cut towns from 15 to about 8 to 10 (end condition already "up to 1 more"), and give every town one new twist (e.g. a puzzle or a map choice) instead of the same 4-option card. Tell the player at the start how many screens in total.
5. Make the result transparent: show the numeric points for each interest next to the radar (e.g. "Thinker 8, Organiser 7, Creator 2"), label the 1 to 5 scale ("1 = Not me, 5 = Very me"), show puzzle results as "3 of 4 correct" instead of "Strong", and put a link "How we got this result" at the top of the result, not at the bottom. Also change US dollars to rupees or state "US data" in large text, and drop metaphors in rules (e.g. "Where is your mind right now?" -> "Which subject group are you thinking of: Science, Commerce, ...?").
