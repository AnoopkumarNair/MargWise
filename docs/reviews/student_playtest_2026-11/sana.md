## 4. Top 5 moments that lost her
1. **The repeating town loop.** Every one of 15 towns is the same: a one-line problem, four options, "Light the lamp · keep my oil", then a one-line "calm" result. Example town text: "Fog has settled on the mountain pass, the signal lamp is dim..." then four options. By town 4 she can predict the next screen.
2. **"Second look · 1 to 8 more" (Orchard Village, again).** Text: "The lamp at Orchard Village is flickering, as if it wants a second look." It repeats the same four options she already answered, and the counter changed from "Place 6 of 15 · 2 to 9 more" to "Second look · 1 to 8 more", then "2 to 9 more". It looks like a quiz re-ask, not story.
3. **Her choices do not change the outcome.** Fog Pass: she picked "Lift the lost travellers' spirits" and "Map where the fog is thickest", and the result said "Warden Karan: 'Nobody was left behind in my pass tonight.'" (the Count option, which she did not pick). Salt Harbour: she picked "Work out from tides and wind" and "Paint a big board", and Captain Rao said "That engine has not purred like that in years" (the engine option). The finale says it outright: "Whatever you chose along the way, every lamp caught."
4. **Stakes never exist.** Every result is "The orchard is calm", "The market hums", "The herbs will sleep well tonight". Nothing goes wrong, nobody is unhappy, no lamp fails to light. Even "Light the lamp · keep my oil" has no cost: the two flasks never run out. Ila says "my flask is nearly empty" but that is not something Sana can feel.
5. **The mystery has no payoff.** Intro: "its lamplighter vanished three days ago, leaving only a torn note." Every keeper says "She [did a job like the one I offered you]." Finale: "She never settled on one kind of work. She tried many, so that you could find yours." The torn note is never revisited. No answer to why she vanished. Sana (fan-fic writer) reads this as a cop-out. Secondary: the lock puzzles (rotated shapes, "A flask costs ₹40..., 1 free for every 4") break the spell, and the pre-game form and 486-word privacy text delays the story.

## 5. Top 3 moments that worked
1. **Intro cards**: "Its lamplighter vanished three days ago, leaving only a torn note: 'A lamp burns best for hands that love their work.'" Short, with "Skip the story" available. Good hook, good voice.
2. **Ila at Night Bazaar** (shot_050): "Everyone remembers her differently. A mechanic, a mapmaker, a storyteller. What was she looking for?" This is the one line that turns the game into a mystery. Also Ila handing over the lantern at the end ("Take mine. I will keep the harbour lamp burning.") landed.
3. **Law page "See which choices pointed here"**: "Printers' Quarter: you picked 'Launch a new newspaper...' (Persuader) and 'Investigate how the old press works...' (Thinker)." This is where her choices finally matter, and it explains the result in her own words. Also Printers' Quarter (town 11) lit her up: newspaper and investigation are her actual dream job.

## 6. Story: did she care?
Partly, for about 3 screens. She cared about Ila and the missing lamplighter. Then the plot stopped moving: Ila appears only at towns 3, 6, 9, 15 (about every third town), and each keeper gives a one-sentence memory that is basically a flattering description of the option set ("She sat with the tired travellers..."). Jyo the firefly has no lines after the intro.

What would make her care:
- Let one of her choices change what the keeper says about the lamplighter, so each memory becomes a clue she can piece together.
- Give the mystery a real answer (she is at the lighthouse, or a letter to Ila with a specific reason).
- Let a lamp fail or cost something once. Stakes.
- Ila, Jyo and the keepers need distinct voices. Currently they all say a warm, quotable line.

## 7. Questions
- Counted: 15 towns x 4 options (pick 2 each, about 30 picks) + 2 "second look" re-asks + 6 locks + class/board/stream (3 questions) + self-rating + feedback. About 12 to 14 minutes of pure "pick". She would say "too many" at about town 7. The honest count of screens that are a question: 15 + 2 + 6 = 23 before the result.
- Repetitive: the second look (same town twice, quoted above). Also the two-flasks rule ("You have two flasks of oil. Spend them on the two things you would most enjoy: both on one if you love it, or one on each...") repeats on every town screen and again on result screens (it is shown again on the town result, see shot_016).
- Confusing: "Place 7 of 15 · 1 to 8 more", "Place 8 of 15 · up to 7 more", "Second look · 2 to 9 more". Which is it, 15 places or 8 more? Honest guess: the game is adaptive (stops early if confident), but Sana sees an unexplained, shifting number.
- Option text mixes the task with a drawback ("...in freezing wind", "...though it may turn out to be junk"). Readable, but it reads as a personality quiz rather than a choice with consequences.
- The word "keep my oil" on the lamp button is unexplained.

## 8. Lock puzzles: fun or exam-like?
Exam-like. Intro line: "The old lamplighter sealed this gate. Jyo says any key you try teaches the lantern a little about you." But the puzzles ("A flask costs ₹40...", "Map is to traveller as recipe is to...", "Which one is the same shape, just turned around?", "What comes next? ↑ → ↓ ← ↑ ?", "Two lamps blink together... 6 seconds and 8 seconds", "Which word means almost the same as 'ambiguous'?") are the same exam items as in any aptitude test with a lamp icon. The same flavour text on every lock. Sana liked the words ones (analogy, ambiguous) and skipped the rotation shape twice. "Click. The lock turns" is a thin reward. Skipping is easy and cheap, which is good. The result screen then says "Puzzle strengths: Numbers Strong, Words Strong, Patterns Strong" even though I skipped shapes: this is fine, but she would ask "Strong compared to what?".

## 10. Her 5 concrete suggestions, ranked
1. **Make choices change the story, even a little.** The keeper's reply must reflect what you picked. Fog Pass: if you pick "Lift the travellers' spirits" the Warden says "She sang with us" or whatever, not the Count line. Let the lamplighter's "memories" depend on your two picks so the player assembles a clue chain.
2. **Pay off the mystery.** Answer "why did she vanish?" with something specific and quotable (e.g. she was at the lighthouse all along, or left the "torn note" for Ila for a reason). Right now the end is "She tried many, so that you could find yours", a moral, not a solution. Reuse the torn note in the finale.
3. **Cut the repeated town structure and the "Second look".** Max 10 towns, merge the two re-asks into the end (as a "Which lamp do you want to go back to?" choice, not a rerun), and fix the counter ("Place 7 of 15 · 1 to 8 more" is incomprehensible; just say "Lamp 7 of 12").
4. **Give Ila and Jyo more voice, earlier and more often.** Ila first appears after town 3 and then only every 3rd town. Have her react to your actual pick ("You chose to run the camp meeting? She'd have done the same.") and put a stake in front of the player: a flask that really runs low, a lamp that is about to go out.
5. **Make locks part of the story or drop most of them.** "The lamplighter sealed this gate" should be a riddle in her voice that Sana can solve with words (analogies, "ambiguous"), not rotating shapes and "₹40 flask" maths. Also shorten the pre-game: move the 486-word privacy block behind "What we keep, and who sees it" and put the story first.
