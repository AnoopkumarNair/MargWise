## 4. Top 5 moments that lost her
1. **Length vs promise.** Cover and form both say "About 15 minutes. No marks, no wrong answers." The top bar then says "Place 1 of 15 · 7 to 14 more" (shot_012). By Place 5 she had spent about 12 min and the bar said "3 to 10 more". Quote: "Place 5 of 15 · 3 to 10 more". The range is wider than a clear number and reads as "it could be a lot".
2. **The "Second look" repeat.** "Second look · 1 to 8 more / Orchard Village / The lamp at Orchard Village is flickering, as if it wants a second look." Then "Second look · 2 to 9 more": the remaining count goes up. A rule-follower reads this as "I did something wrong".
3. **Exam-style locks inside a story.** "Lock 1 of 2: Numbers ... A flask costs ₹40. The shop gives 1 flask free for every 4 you buy. You need 10 flasks. How much do you pay?" and "Which word means almost the same as "ambiguous"?" Together with "Jyo says any key you try teaches the lantern a little about you", it feels like being tested and judged. The feedback "Click. The lock turns and the gate swings open." does not say right or wrong, so she keeps wondering.
4. **Same screen shape 15 times.** Each town: one setup line, four options with a downside ("with sore arms and wet feet", "in freezing wind"), pick two, "Light the lamp · keep my oil", a reaction card, Next. By town 5 it is a routine with no new rule or surprise.
5. **The plot does not move.** The lamplighter clues ("She roped down these cliffs to check the old lamps...") are one line each and never add up to anything she can guess. Ila appears at town 3, then at 6, 9, 15 with one short line. No mystery, no stakes.

## 5. Top 3 moments that worked
1. **The finale screen** (shot_112): "15 lamps are burning behind you. The lighthouse is lit." with the glowing lighthouse art. This is the only moment with real emotion.
2. **The interest shape** (shot_113): Thinker, Helper, Creator fits her (biology, helping, drawing and poems). The radar chart is clear and she can tap each area. The note "Further from the middle means you enjoyed it more, compared with your own other choices" is reassuring.
3. **The lamplighter lines that touch her interests:** "She taught me to read the clock's gears, then sketched the whole tower on the back of her hand." and "She sat with the sick through a long night, and tested the well water the next morning." The Healing House and Glass Cliffs ("Draw a picture map") options were the ones she picked fastest.

## 6. Story
- Did not care much. She likes the look (cover art, lighthouse), and "A lamp burns best for hands that love their work" is a good line. But the plot is: walk, help, lamp, the keeper says one line about a lamplighter.
- Ila is the only character she could attach to, and she appears 4 times in 15 towns with 2 sentences each. Jyo the firefly says almost nothing after the intro.
- What would make her care (guess, in her voice): Ila should be a real companion who reacts to HER choices ("you stayed with the sick travellers, just like her"); one visible clue board or sketchbook that fills in as she goes (she draws); and the lamplighter's identity should become guessable around the middle. There is no writing or poems choice anywhere, so the thing she loves most never shows up (Printers' Quarter had proofread, launch a newspaper, press, letter style: no "write a poem").

## 7. Questions
- **How many:** 15 towns x 2 picks = about 30 choices, plus 8 puzzles, plus class/board/stream. Too many for a 15-minute promise; she felt it from town 5.
- **Repetitive:** every town has the same four-options-pick-two pattern (see 4.4); the Second look repeat of Orchard Village (see 4.2).
- **Confusing:**
  - "Place 1 of 15 · 7 to 14 more" (is it 15 or 7 to 14 more?).
  - "You have two flasks of oil. Spend them on the two things you would most enjoy: both on one if you love it, or one on each. Pick what you really like, not a bit of everything." That is a long rule repeated on every town screen. After the first, she reads it again each time because she is careful.
  - Clicking "Light the lamp · keep my oil" with nothing chosen gives "Tap again to keep your oil and light". She did not know what that meant (this happened at town 4, from a misclick).
  - Several options hide the "good" part behind a "though" (e.g. "Settle the argument with a deal both traders accept, though neither is fully happy"). She worries about what the "right" attitude is.

## 8. Lock puzzles: fun or exam-like?
Exam-like. The numbers (discount), words (analogy, synonym), shapes (rotate, not flip) and patterns are the same kinds of items as in school and entrance tests. "A flipped copy does not count" sounds like a trap. After picking a key, there is no right/wrong signal, so she keeps wondering. The shape puzzle took about a minute of real thinking the second time (6-cell shape), and there is no timer, so that part is fine. She did not use "Skip this lock": skipping feels like admitting a failure. The result page then says "Puzzle strengths ... Numbers: Strong, Words: Strong, Shapes: Strong, Patterns: Strong", which confirms they were being scored (I answered all correctly). That would please her, but a student who got them wrong would feel judged, and the hint "They do not change your interest shape" comes too late.

## 9. Result: did it fit? would she share it?
- **Fit:** Strong. Shape: Thinker, Helper, Creator. Fields: Understanding people (psychology), Technology and data, Health and medicine. Health and medicine is third and "close to the next one", which is a good honest answer for a girl torn between medicine and "something else". Her top field (psychology) was a surprise but plausible. Nothing suggests drawing or writing as a path (Arts and design and Media are only in "All 16 fields"); the Observatory "Paint the night sky" choice was reported as "A choice that pointed somewhere else", which she liked.
- **Understanding:** The radar and the three cards are clear. "See which choices pointed here" is a very good transparency feature for a student worried about being right.
- **Problems on the field page (2,200 words):** US pay ("$244k a year"), "AI exposure high" for Doctor, Nurse, Pharmacist, and "Matches come from US job profiles". For a Chennai Class 10 student these numbers are not useful, and "high AI exposure" on medicine could worry her or her parents.
- **Share:** Yes, she would show "Summary for parents" to her parents (one page, "Health and medicine (Science with Biology (PCB))"). Small issues: "Board: cbse" in lowercase; the summary lists "very high AI exposure, in use now" next to many careers, which could scare a parent.

## 10. Her 5 concrete suggestions, ranked
1. **Make the length honest and visible.** Replace "Place 1 of 15 · 7 to 14 more" with one fixed number ("Place 4 of 10") and say the real time on the cover, or cut to about 8 to 10 towns. The counter must never go up ("1 to 8 more" then "2 to 9 more").
2. **Drop or relabel the "Second look" screen.** If it must stay, say "Same village, one more quick look. Your earlier answer stays." and do not count it as a new place.
3. **Make the locks feel like part of the story, not school.** Use story items (count lamps, match a lantern shape), say plainly before the first one: "These are just for fun and do not change your result", and give some response (even "Jyo is not telling"). Remove the numbers-and-words quiz look (₹ discount, synonym of "ambiguous").
4. **Give the story a spine.** Let Ila walk with you and react to your picks in one line; add a clue board that fills in; reveal who the lamplighter is. Add one town or option for writing (poems, journal, stories) and one for drawing a body or a plant, since those are what kids like Meera care about.
5. **Shorten text on every screen.** Cut the long "You have two flasks of oil..." paragraph on each town to one line after town 1; trim the 486-word sign-up page (privacy text behind a "What we keep" link); show Indian rupee pay or no pay at all on career cards instead of US dollars.
