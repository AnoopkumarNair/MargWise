# Arjun (persona playtest report)

Viewport note: play.mjs only supports phone (390x844), tablet, laptop. It has no custom size. I used VP=phone 390x844, NOT 360x640. On a real 360x640 low-end Android everything below would be tighter: the town screen (4 big cards plus a pinned "Light the lamp" button) and the shape lock (tiny cells) would likely need scrolling. That is a guess, not tested. Network throttling was also not simulated (screens were served from localhost).

## 1. Persona
Arjun, 15, Class 10, Maharashtra State Board (Marathi medium till Class 8), village near Nashik, farmer parents, first in family to go to college. Likes drip-irrigation videos, cricket, helping at the ration shop. Father's old Android, patchy data. ~130 wpm English. Trusts teachers, tries hard, gets confused easily, wonders if careers are "for people like him".

## 2. Did they finish? Quit point
Arjun would have played to roughly the middle and then drifted off. He is dutiful, so he does not quit angrily; he stops when the phone is needed or the data runs out.
WOULD QUIT HERE: screen = lock ("Lock 1 of 2: Shapes", second time, start of Chapter III), after 8 places and 6 locks. Counter said "Place 8 of 15 · up to 7 more". Estimated real time at that point: about 14 minutes (gate form and 358-word notice ~3 min, welcome and 4 intro cards ~2 min, ~9 town screens at ~70 s = ~10 min incl. one revisit, 6 locks ~4 min, minus overlap; estimate 13-15 min). The game promises "About 15 minutes", and at 15 minutes he was only half way.
Reason, in his words: "Madam said 15 minutes. I have done 15 minutes and it still says seven more places. And the same orchard village came again. Baba needs the phone, and data is low. The shape puzzle is like a hard exam question."
He kept going as a tester. Full run to the result: about 28-30 real minutes (guess).
Towns done at quit: 8 (9 town screens counting the Orchard repeat). Puzzles done at quit: 6 of 8.

## 4. Top 5 moments that lost him
1. Town 7 is Orchard Village again. Screen text: "The lamp at Orchard Village is flickering, as if it wants a second look." Same four options as town 1 ("Test apple blends...", "Run the press...", "Sell the fresh juice...", "Teach visiting children..."). He thinks it is a bug or that he is being asked to repeat.
2. The shape lock appears twice: "Which one is the same shape, just turned around? A flipped copy does not count." Four tiny block shapes on a phone. Never explained what "flipped" means. Feels like a hard school test, and the second one comes at the half-way point.
3. The verbal puzzles: "Map is to traveller as recipe is to ..." and "Which word means almost the same as "gleam"?" He reads English at 130 wpm and does not know analogy questions. The game then says "The lock holds this time, so Jyo guides you round the side." He feels he failed.
4. Length with no end in sight: "Place 8 of 15 · up to 7 more", and the welcome said "About 15 minutes." Each town screen is the same loop, and the progress bar goes up slowly. The oil-flask rule text repeated at every town result: "You have two flasks of oil. Spend them on the two things you would most enjoy: both on one if you love it, or one on each. Pick what you really like, not a bit of everything. There are no right answers and nothing is marked." (50 words, shown first town, and again on the result screen of each town.)
5. The sign-up page: 358 words, with a long "What we keep, and who sees it" block, "I am 18 or older, or my parent or guardian knows about this and agrees," and a second screen "Is a parent or guardian with you?" He is 15 and alone on his father's phone; he taps yes without understanding. Also the privacy block is full-length on the page in small print.

## 5. Top 3 moments that worked
1. The cover and the lit lighthouse (finale): "The lighthouse is lit." with golden rays and "15 lamps are burning behind you." This is a real reward and the only emotional peak.
2. The shop-price lock: "A flask costs ₹40. The shop gives 1 flask free for every 4 you buy. You need 10 flasks. How much do you pay?" Rupees, a shop, a deal: it is his ration-shop world. He felt clever.
3. Garden and Salt Market towns ("Test soils and seeds to grow healthier plants", "Weigh what salt is left so each trader's loss is clear", "Haul the salt sacks off the road") and then the fields page: "Agriculture and environment ... Start here." He feels seen when his top field is farming. The "See which choices pointed here" panel helps a little.

## 6. Story
He understood the setup (lamplighter vanished, Ila looks for her, Jyo the firefly) but did not care much. Why: the characters are only text cards; Ila appears after town 3 though the intro says "Walk the road with Ila"; there are no clues or mystery to solve; the lamplighter is never found; the ending ("She never settled on one kind of work. She tried many, so that you could find yours.") explains the mystery away without an answer. For him a mystery would work if each village gave one clue ("Granny Pari: 'Last autumn she pressed apples with us...'" is the best line and actually does this). What would make him care: Ila speaking to him after each town in a line about HIS choice; the same village people (like his own village) asking for help; a clue that he can find only by picking farm or shop actions; a Marathi line or two. The words "lamplighter", "lighthouse", "ferry", "quay", "harness" are not in his daily English.

## 7. Questions
- 15 places x pick 2 of 4 is about 30 choices, plus 8 puzzles, plus the 3-item class/board/stream page, plus the 5-point "Does this shape sound like you?" and "Rate each area yourself" on the result page. For him, "too many" starts after about town 6 because the four choices look alike.
- Repetitive: the same instruction paragraph on every town result; the same loop (pick, pick, "Light the lamp", "Next"). The Orchard Village repeat.
- Confusing, quoted:
  - "Spend them on the two things you would most enjoy: both on one if you love it, or one on each." (what is a flask? what are "two things"?)
  - "Light the lamp · keep my oil" (the button text; he does not know what "keep my oil" does)
  - "Where is your mind right now?" (stream question, he reads it as "where is my mind")
  - Heading "Two quick things first" over three questions.
  - Choice wording with a negative tag on every option: "...though it may take days...", "...even if some liked the old one", "...with oily hands and no one to ask for help". They read as warnings, not as things he would try; one clause per option is a lot to read at 130 wpm.
  - Choices that assume city or club life: "Recruit new members for the stargazing club", "Launch a new newspaper for the quarter", "Paint a big board", "Win over the passing buyers".
- Words: he understands a few options much better than others (press, engine, soil, weigh, haul). "Redraw", "proofread", "timetable", "chimes" slow him.

## 8. Lock puzzles
Mostly exam-like. Fun only when they use his world (the ₹40 flask sum; LCM of 6 and 8 as blinking lamps is fine but plain maths). The word analogy and "gleam" synonym test English, not interest, and make him feel behind. The shape-rotation puzzle is not fun on a small screen. The wording "Pick a key" and "teaches the lantern a little about you" does not tell him he is being tested on thinking skill. When he is wrong, "The lock holds this time, so Jyo guides you round the side" is kind but he still knows he missed it. Result later says "Words: Steady" and "Shapes: Strong" even though he guessed on shapes, so the page overstates it (my guess about scoring: he got 1 shape right by luck; I did not check the scoring).

## 9. Result
Fit: yes, better than expected. Top three Thinker, Builder, Organiser and "Agriculture and environment" as first field. That matches farm technology, repairing, and the ration shop. The page also said "You were leaning towards Science. Your shape agrees." He understood the three cards but not the radar chart.
Reachability and city/money assumptions (important):
- Careers show US pay and US jobs: Forester "$76k a year", Farm or Agribusiness Manager "$90k", "Jobs 2035 ▶ +2.2% in the US". These are not real for him. The note "Matches come from US job profiles. Indian courses, exams and pay can differ" is buried far down the page. The "AI exposure" labels (moderate, high, very high) are hard to read for a student and mean nothing for a village.
- The first careers are scientist roles (Conservation Scientist, Soil and Plant Scientist, Animal Scientist) with a 4-year degree. Nothing shows a middle route like a Diploma in Agriculture, ITI, Krishi Vidyapeeth, KVK, drip irrigation technician or agri-tech business. Not one thing about cost, fees, hostel or scholarships (Maharashtra EBC/OBC fee waivers, Mahadbt), so he cannot tell if his family could afford it.
- School path: "Science, with Biology or Maths. Agriculture is also an elective in some boards." Exams: "CUET (ICAR-UG), run by NTA for ICAR..." With State board chosen, there is no mention of Maharashtra, MHT-CET, state agricultural universities by name (MPKV Rahuri is near him), or Marathi-medium help. The "State / other" tab did not change anything he could see.
- The salary block says "salaried graduates in India earn about ₹32,683 a month" and is truncated with "…" and "not yet checked against MoSPI". For him ₹32,000 is real money and the only number he trusts, but it is in the "More: pay in India, plan B, colleges" fold.
- Plan B text: "B.Sc in botany, zoology or environmental science" and "NIRF rankings" assume a city college and internet.
- "Try this week: Grow plants, join an eco club, or visit a farm or nursery" is good, but "eco club" is a school-club idea he may not have; he already lives on a farm, so it reads oddly.
Would he share it: with a parent, yes, if the Summary for parents page were printed or sent by WhatsApp (it has "Print or save as PDF" and a "Student code"). It is long (403 words) and all English. He would show his teacher first.

## 10. His 5 suggestions, ranked
1. Cut the length: 15 places is too many for a half-hour of data on a poor phone. Show 8 places, or end when the shape is already clear (the results page itself says some areas were close, so you could stop early or add 2-3 more places only if tied). Never show "7 to 14 more" at place 1.
2. Never show the same place twice in the main run, or if you must, say "Back to Orchard Village. Do you still feel the same?" and name why. Remove the Orchard repeat from the first half.
3. Replace the English-only puzzles (analogy, synonym) and the tiny shape-rotation puzzle with ones using everyday things: shop money, weights, fields, water, cricket scores, a picture pattern. Show one puzzle at a time with a plain "this is a puzzle, not marks" line. Make the shape puzzle bigger (the cells are tiny at 390 px wide) and say "turn, not flip" with a picture.
4. Show Indian numbers and routes first. Put India pay (the ₹ figure), the state exam (MHT-CET or the state agriculture admission), fees and scholarships, and a diploma/ITI/Krishi Vidyapeeth route above the US pay and AI badges. Hide US pay in a closed panel or remove it. Say plainly on the careers page "These are for the US. See India below."
5. Make the story do more per screen: Ila should speak after each town in one line about his pick, one clue per chapter, and an ending that answers the mystery. Shorter, plainer sentences, Marathi/Hindi option for the story and the choices ("Light the lamp · keep my oil" button and the 50-word oil rule are the worst). Remove the repeated oil paragraph on result screens.
