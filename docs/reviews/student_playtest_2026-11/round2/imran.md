# Playtest report: Imran (round 2, phone 390x844, port 9208)

## 1. Persona
Imran, 14, Class 9 state board, Hyderabad. Urdu-medium until Class 6, helps in his uncle's biryani shop, loves cooking videos and cricket. Low confidence about studies, worried his marks are not good enough for "big careers".

Assumption: the consent screen says students under 18 need a parent or guardian to agree. I played as if his uncle (guardian) had agreed, so I ticked the box and chose "A parent has agreed". A 14-year-old who answers "Not yet. I will ask first." is stopped at this screen. That is a real fork for this age group.

## 2. FINISHED: yes (sharpened, 12 towns)
- Draft: 8 towns (Orchard Village, Glass Cliffs, Herbalists' Garden, Salt Market, River Camp, Night Bazaar, Festival Square, Clockmaker's Lane).
- Sharpened: 4 more (Observatory Hill, Healing House, Lantern Bridge, Fog Pass). Stopped with "Stop and see my result" once the sharpen counter said "about a minute each" and he had reached 12 towns.
- Then: class, board and stream questions, the 12-lamps finale, the result, and the Sports and fitness field card.
- Estimated time: about 20 to 25 minutes. Most of it is reading. The sign-up, consent and privacy text took about 4 minutes on its own. Ila's and the lamplighter's beats are short, but the choice cards are long to read in English. The promised "8 to 10 minutes" is clearly too short for this student.
- Tool actions used: 69 (about 7.5 min of tool time).

## 4. What kept him going and what nearly lost him

Kept going:
1. The choices matched his life. "Film a short climb video for the group, redoing shots as the light shifts" and "Coach the junior team for the festival match" read like him.
2. The result said where each choice pointed. Fog Pass: "you picked 'Mend the dim signal lamp...' (Builder)". He felt noticed.
3. The field card "Sports and fitness" had cricket in it ("Coaching, sports science and fitness careers are much more common").

Nearly lost him:
1. The draft screen: "No clear shape yet. Not much stood out in 8 towns." This made him feel tested and failing. He chose "Sharpen it" only because he wanted a result.
2. The sharpen loop: "Sharpening: up to 7 more towns, about a minute each." Each sharpened town cost him about 1.5 to 2 minutes of reading. "Each lamp makes your picture a little sharper" did not show him progress, so he stopped at 12 towns.
3. The class, board and stream questions came after the sharpen loop, before the result. "Skip anything you like" was on screen, but there was no skip button for the form. He had to answer all three.

## 5. Did the story and reactions notice him? Did the mystery land?
- Reactions: yes, when they were about his choices. "You went to the people first", "You split your oil. Fair enough." and "You saw a chance and took it." felt like someone was watching. These are good lines.
- Story: thin for him. He came for the choices, not the mystery. The lamplighter's letter ("The road was never mine. Choose yours.") and "She was never lost" were read once and did not hook him. It felt a little like a speech at the end.
- Mystery ending: it did not land as a mystery for him. It landed as a pep talk. The line "Her footprints and yours, side by side" is the closest it came.

## 6. Does the result fit? Would he share it?
- Fit: partly. Builder fits (he fixes things at the shop). Helper fits (he serves customers and travellers). Creator fits the video choice. He finds it right but "rough" because it said "A rough first draft" and "Does this shape sound like you?" showed up only after the big shape.
- Mixed message: the result says "A rough first draft... the order of the top three is not fixed", but the field card says "Clear signal: several of your choices agree." A 14-year-old with low confidence reads both and is not sure which one to believe.
- Share: he would show the Sports and fitness card to his uncle and cricket friends. He would not share the radar chart or the "rough first draft" text. He would not send the whole thing to his parents without a line about what it is for.
- Careers: the field page is about 1,000 words in one screen. He read the first line and the India pay line ("salaried graduates ... about 32,683 a month"). That number made him think "big careers are far away." Most career cards said "No India pay figure for this job yet."

## 7. Bugs or layout problems
- Result screen (shot: screenshots not kept): the line "In school this often shows up in: Psychology, Biology, languages, Physics..." is hidden under the fixed bottom button "See where this can lead". It cannot be read without scrolling, and the button covers it.
- Tapping step 3 "Careers and path" in the result header opened one field (Sports and fitness) directly, not a list of fields. Going back to the list needs "Back to all fields" at the bottom of a long page. Worth checking if that is the intended path.
- Field page (shot: screenshots not kept): one screen of about 1,000 words. The "Your choices at" and "Choices that pointed here" blocks are good, but the career cards and pay text are dense for a student reading in English.
- Consent wall: a 14-year-old has only two choices, "A parent has agreed" or "Not yet. I will ask first." The second ends the session with no next step. Consider a message such as "Ask a parent, then come back."
- Class/board/stream questions show "Skip anything you like" but there is no skip control. Choosing nothing did not seem to allow "Show my interest shape" (not tested).
- Sharpen counter says "about a minute each"; real time for this student was closer to 2 minutes each, with reading.
