## 4. What kept him going / what nearly lost him

Kept him going:
1. Choice cards with real business actions: "Pitch your stall to buyers", "Start a small juice stall ... risk unsold crates", "Make a short video for the waiting crowd". He picked these three of the four times he had a choice like them.
2. Quick, sortable results: "Your top three: Persuader, Creator, Thinker", then a field card that said "Business and finance ... Fits your Persuader". "Ok, there it is."
3. The school path for CBSE Commerce (Class 11 to 12, CA / B.Com / CMA). This is the first thing that felt like it was for him.

Nearly lost him:
1. Welcome screen: "Once a year the valley lights every lamp on the old road... Ila and a firefly named Jyo need your help." He said "oh no, it's a bedtime story." This is the "made for kids" trigger.
2. Lamp result lines: "Jyo smiles." "Jyo watches." "Granny Pari: Sweet juice, and now a lamp." He read them as filler, and two of them felt babyish.
3. Sign-up wall: "Data: what we keep and who sees it" (about 320 words). He almost left. He skimmed to the checkbox.
4. Field page: the AI-exposure section repeated "What these mean ... Very high AI exposure, in use now" seven times. He nearly quit scrolling. "Why is this the same paragraph seven times."

## 5. Did it notice him?
Partly. The lamp replies pick up his choice labels ("People followed your lead tonight", "Jyo likes how you keep order", "Some of each. Jyo smiles."). These are generic. Kabir did not feel seen by them. The field page did notice him: "You were leaning towards Commerce. Your shape agrees", and the "Choices that pointed here" list named the River Camp and Night Bazaar picks. He noticed that list and liked it, because it showed why.

The mystery ending: it landed a little. "She was never lost. She went ahead to make room for you." and the lamplighter's letter "The road was never mine. Choose yours." He thought it was a nice line, but he did not care who Jyo was. The "Who is Jyo" moment did not pull him into the mystery.

## 6. Does the result fit? Would he share it?
- Fit: yes, mostly. Persuader and Creator fit a D2C founder with a short-video habit. Thinker felt like a stretch ("I work things out with numbers, sure"). "Business and finance" was a clear hit.
- Share: probably not in its current form. He might send the screenshot of the "Business and finance" card to one friend with "ok this is kind of me." He would not post the radar chart or the story. The firefly and the lamps would be the thing he left out.

## 7. Bugs and layout problems

1. Repeated text on the field page. The "What these mean" block and "Real AI use seen so far" text repeat for each career (7 times for "very high, in use"). Screenshot: screenshots not kept (field page, top); scroll further in the same run for the repeats.
2. Duplicate text in the plan B block: "If CA is not for you: B.Com with CMA, CS or CFA alongside..." appears twice in a row. Also "Compare colleges with the official NIRF rankings" appears as a button label and again as a paragraph. Same field page, after the school path.
3. Inconsistent signal: the result screen shows Organiser under "Less strong for now", but the Business and finance field card shows "Organiser ●●○" as a matching area, and the choices list tags a bazaar pick as Organiser. A student may ask why an area that was "less strong" is listed as a match.

The "not yet checked" note is visible to a student, and the space before the comma is a typo. Screenshot: shot_051.png area near "Pay in India".

He did this without asking anyone, which is the realistic path for a 16-year-old. This is a product risk, not only a UI note.
