## 4. What kept him going, and what nearly lost him

Kept going:
1. "Herbalists' Garden" (town 7): "Test the soil with a sensor and pick better seeds". It matched his drip-irrigation videos and he picked it at once.
2. The town choices were short and concrete ("Fix the juice press and run it all day, with aching arms", "Coach the junior team for the festival match"). He reacted to cricket and repairs, and to hands-on work.
3. The reveal tying his choices back to the story: "15 lamps are burning behind you. The lighthouse is lit." He liked seeing the lamps count up.

Nearly lost him:
1. Consent screen: "Students under 18 should not do it without that." and "Not yet. I will ask first." He would have stopped there if no parent was around.
2. Field card for Health and medicine: "Matches come from US job profiles. Indian courses, exams and pay can differ" and "Real AI use seen so far (Anthropic, 2025 usage) covers about 2% of this job." He could not tell what "AI exposure: high" meant for him, and the careers (physiotherapist, dentist, radiologic technologist) did not look like anyone he knew.
3. "Pay in India: what we know ... (Periodic Labour Force Survey 2025, MoSPI press note and India Labour Observatory tables, not yet checked against MoSPI)." Too much to read on a slow phone, and "not yet checked" would make him doubt the number.

## 5. Did it feel like they noticed the student?

Partly.
- Yes: the "Choices that pointed here" lists quoted his own choices back to him, e.g. "Herbalists' Garden: you picked 'Test the soil with a sensor...' (Thinker)". This was the moment he most felt seen.
- Yes: the NPCs reacted to his picks ("Her lamp here is still warm", "Ila: Her footprints and yours, side by side").
- No: the field and career cards are generic. Nothing mentions Nashik, Marathi, the ration shop, drip irrigation or cricket. The Agriculture field, the closest match to his drip-irrigation interest, was not in his top three ("Close call"). He would ask why.
- The mystery ending mostly landed. "She was never lost. She went ahead to make room for you." He liked that. He did not fully follow who "she" was until Ila explained, and he would have liked a clearer "the lamplighter is your teacher" line.
- Parent summary says "Student name (you can edit it)", which he would find a nice touch.

## 6. Does the result fit? Would they share it?

- Fit: partly. Helper, Builder and Organiser feel right for a boy who fixes things and helps at the shop. Science as the stream feels right. The careers did not, because they came from US profiles and he wanted soil and farm work.
- Share: he would show his father the "Summary for parents" page, since his father is the one who would need to agree. He would not share the game with friends, because it looked like school homework and took too long.

## 7. Bugs and layout problems

No JavaScript page errors were logged in the run. Layout was clean at 390 px width. Issues:
- Length: 2.5 times the promised "8 to 10 minutes" (see section 2). The sharpen round added about 8 minutes on its own.
- The "All 16 fields, by stream" row is not in the driver's list of buttons. I opened it with a script call, so I could not confirm that a real tap works. It may be missed by keyboard and screen-reader users if it is a plain div.
- The field card is about 2,000 words for Health, and about 1,400 for Agriculture. Too long for a phone. The career items with "AI exposure" and "Real AI use seen so far (Anthropic, 2025 usage) covers about 2% of this job" are jargon.
- "Matches come from US job profiles" and "Career fit uses US job profiles, so it is a rough guide for India" appear on every field card. Clear, but it reads as a warning.
- Pay figure: "not yet checked against MoSPI" is jargon, and it reads as unfinished.
