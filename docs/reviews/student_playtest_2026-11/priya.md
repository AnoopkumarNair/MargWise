## 4. Top 5 moments that lost them
1. Orchard Village shown twice. Text: "The lamp at Orchard Village is flickering, as if it wants a second look." Same four choices (Test apple blends / Teach visiting children / Run the press / Sell the fresh juice), shuffled. She reads it as the game running out of content.
2. The first lock. "A flask costs ₹40. The shop gives 1 flask free for every 4 you buy. You need 10 flasks. How much do you pay?" After the intro promised "No marks, no wrong answers", this is a school sum with four options.
3. The shape lock. "Which one is the same shape, just turned around? A flipped copy does not count." (shot_034.png). Needs concentration, small shapes, wrong thing to ask on a bumpy bus. The Skip button is at the very bottom edge or offscreen on a phone ("#lkSkip [offscreen: scroll]" in the driver), so she nearly misses it.
4. The sign-up. 358 words on the first form screen (including "What we keep, and who sees it"), then a parent-consent screen: "Students under 18 should not do it alone." She is 17 and plays alone on a bus; this tells her she is doing it wrong. Then a login code she must save before she has seen anything.
5. The wait before the payoff. Nothing about sport, physio or her plan appears until after 11 towns and 4 lock sets. The only sign that the game respects her plan is Healing House (town 3) and Glass Cliffs (town 2); the first town is apple juice. Also the intro text: "Once a year, on the Night of Lamps, the valley lights one lamp for every road a person might take." Nothing in the first screens says "tell us what you like and we will show careers like physio".

## 5. Top 3 moments that worked
1. "Sports and fitness" as the number 1 field, then "Health and medicine" as number 2, on the "Where this can lead" screen. She feels seen. (She hit "Open" immediately.)
2. Healing House (town 3): "Sit with the sick travellers and talk with them", "Lead the clinic team tonight". First town that felt like her plan. Also Glass Cliffs (climbing/leading).
3. The Health page showing "Physiotherapist" as the first career, and the exams step in plain words: "NEET-UG (run by NTA) is the entrance test for MBBS, BDS, BAMS, BHMS, BUMS and BVSc... most nursing, pharmacy and physiotherapy admissions run through state routes that differ." Plus plan B text: "If the NEET result does not go your way: allied health degrees (B.Sc Nursing, physiotherapy...". That answers her NEET question honestly. Also the line "Only a few become full-time athletes. Coaching, sports science and fitness careers are much more common." and "Choices that pointed here" with her own picks quoted back.

The keeper lines ("She sat with the sick through a long night, and tested the well water the next morning.") are cute but she did not care; they are not about her. ## 8. Lock puzzles
Exam-like. The labels ("Numbers", "Words", "Shapes", "Patterns") are literally the school aptitude test. She answered 3 of 8, skipped 5 (two shape locks, LCM, vocabulary "ambiguous", number series 2,4,3,6,5,10,9). Skip works and says "No problem. Jyo knows another way round", which is kind. But the framing "Jyo says any key you try teaches the lantern a little about you" does not change that it's a quiz. The arrow pattern was the only one she did not mind. She would NOT know they are optional unless she sees the Skip button (it is below the fold on the shape lock).

## 9. Result
Fit: good. Builder, Helper, Persuader, with "Sports and fitness" first and "Health and medicine" second matches kabaddi + physio. She understood the radar and the three cards. "Does this shape sound like you? 1-5" is fine. Why it works: field names are plain.
Problems for her: (a) Physiotherapist is not in the Sports page careers (Fitness Trainer, Professional Athlete, Coach or Sports Trainer) and only shows in the Health page, so she has to click "Health" even though the game ranked it second; sports science/physio crossover is not mentioned on the Sports page except as "sports science courses" in plan B. (b) All careers show US pay in dollars ("$103k", "$47k") which means nothing to her; the India pay is a national all-jobs average (about Rs 32,683 a month for salaried graduates), explicitly "not these roles". She asked about pay and got no physio pay in rupees. (c) AI exposure labels ("high" for Physiotherapist) may scare her, with no context. (d) Pages are very long: the Sports page is 1095 words, the Health page 2184 words. She would read the first card and the exams box, then stop. (e) The Health page says "Science with Biology (PCB) is the usual choice" which she already has, good. (f) College names are not given: "NIRF does not rank this area" on Sports; no named physio colleges in what I read (I did not open Compare colleges on the Health page, so I cannot say). Would she share it with a parent? Probably the shape picture and "Sports and fitness" with her friends or her coach; the parent summary button exists ("Summary for parents") but I did not open it. A parent might like the NEET/plan B honesty, but "US pay" and "draft" labels ("Prototype content. Careers and school paths are drafts") undercut trust (guess).

## 10. Five concrete suggestions, ranked
1. Cut the walk to about 6 towns (or stop at a confident result) and never show the same town twice. "Place 1 of 15 . 7 to 14 more" is a promise she reads as 15 chores; if the game needs more data, show the re-visit as a clearly different scene rather than the same four orchard choices.
2. Ask early, in one tap, "Do you already have a plan?" (a plain list of fields including Sports and fitness, Health and medicine) and show that field's real card at once, then use the walk to confirm it. That is the only way a student like Priya sees that the game respects her plan in the first 2 minutes.
3. Make the locks optional by default and out of the story flow: replace the four forced lock pairs with a single "Want to try puzzles? Skip all" choice, and put the Skip button where a thumb can reach (not below the fold on the shape lock). Do not call them "Numbers / Words / Shapes".
4. Fix the result for an Indian student: show rupee pay for the named job (e.g. physiotherapist), link physio from the Sports page ("Sports physio is under Health and medicine"), name two or three admission routes and example colleges, and collapse the AI-exposure and O*NET text behind a tap. Cut the Sports/Health pages to a first screen of 150 words.
5. Shorten the front door: put the 358-word privacy text and the parent-consent screen behind a link, change "Students under 18 should not do it alone" (she is 17, on a bus) to something that lets her carry on, and drop the intro story / chapter beats to one line each (or skip them by default) since she skipped the story and skimmed Ila three times.
