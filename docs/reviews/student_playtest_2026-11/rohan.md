# Rohan playtest report (tablet 820x1180, port 9105)

Time estimates are mine (word count at 80-100 wpm plus thinking), not driver time. Where I say what Rohan would take in, I am guessing from the persona.

## 1. Persona
Rohan, 15, Class 10, Hindi-medium state board, Indore. Moving to English-medium Class 11. Probable dyslexia, reads English at about 80-100 wpm, loses his place in long lines. Loves fixing phones and motors, quick with shop money maths. Plays on the family tablet. Gives up quietly rather than ask.

## 2. Did they finish? Quit point
He plays through the 15 places (the choices are short and have pictures), but he quits at the result, on the field page.
- WOULD QUIT HERE (first real quit): screen "Second look · 1 to 8 more, Orchard Village" (action #50). Done so far: 6 towns (the 6th is Glass Cliffs), 4 lock puzzles. Estimated real time about 17-19 minutes. The game promised "About 15 minutes."
  - Reason, in his words: "Mujhe yeh already kiya. Phir se wahi apple wala? Kitna aur hai?" He had been told "15 places" and was told "Place 6 of 15", then the game showed town 1 again as a "second look". The counter then read "1 to 8 more" and later "up to 7 more", which he cannot work out.
- Second quit chance, certain: the field page (action #111) is 1,724 words, with US dollar pay, "AI exposure very high" and "Matches come from US job profiles". He closes it here. Estimated 30-35 minutes in total (about 15 places x 1.7 min, 6 locks x 1 min, 3 min of cover and forms, 3 min of results).
- A tester kept going to the end. He did finish the game, but he would not have.

## 4. Top 5 moments that lost him
1. The second look at Orchard Village. Exact text: "Second look · 1 to 8 more / Orchard Village / The lamp at Orchard Village is flickering, as if it wants a second look." After "Place 6 of 15", he sees town 1 again. Then "The flame holds. Thank you for looking again." He feels the game is not honest about its length. (Counters such as "Place 8 of 15 · up to 7 more" and "Place 7 of 15 · 1 to 8 more" make no sense to him.)
2. The sign-up and consent screen. About 500 words on one screen: "We keep your name, the choices you make in the game and how long each place takes, your puzzle answers... A scrambled version of your connection address is kept for 24 hours only, to stop abuse." He would not read any of it. It also reads like an exam notice, so he feels judged before the game starts.
3. Word locks. "Map is to traveller as recipe is to ..." and "Which word means almost the same as 'gleam'?" Both are tests of English, not of what he likes. After the first, "The lock holds this time, so Jyo guides you round the side." The same message is shown for a wrong shape. "Holds" is also an unusual word. The Numbers lock says "Click. The lock turns and the gate swings open." So he learns that the lock "winning" is correct and "holding" is wrong, even though the game says nothing is marked.
4. Long choice lines from town 5 on. The first towns are short ("Run the press and keep it humming all day"). Later ones add a hard clause: "Draw a picture map of the glowing cliff for the climbers, redrawing it as the light shifts"; "Proofread each page before printing, starting over whenever a mistake slips through"; "Weigh what salt is left so each trader's loss is clear, with the crowd shouting nearby". Four of these, each 15-20 words, with a drawback tacked on. He would lose his place after the comma.
5. The field page. 1,724 words, US pay "$135k a year", "Jobs 2035 +8.3% in the US", "AI exposure very high", "Matches come from US job profiles. Indian courses, exams and pay can differ". For a boy in Indore this is not about his life.

Other words he cannot read or understand (my list, flagged as he would meet them):
- Intro: "lamplighter", "vanished", "torn note", "A lamp burns best for hands that love their work", "lighthouse" (he knows "light" and "house", so he could guess it), "remembers", "keeper"
- Choices: "recruit", "chart" (verb), "herbalists'", "passers-by" (broken over two lines), "proofread", "investigate", "harness", "grumble", "quay", "joint tight", "humming", "keep it humming all day" (idiom), "Dream up", "push for a decision", "Win over", "settle the argument", "Haul", "stall", "stallholders", "cobbles", "purred" (Captain Rao's quote: "That engine has not purred like that in years."), "Oil to her elbows" (idiom)

## 5. Top 3 moments that worked
1. Lock 1, Numbers: "A flask costs Rs 40. The shop gives 1 flask free for every 4 you buy. You need 10 flasks. How much do you pay?" This is his father's shop. He gets it in seconds and feels clever. The "6 and 8 seconds, when do they blink together" lock is also easy for him. Best two minutes of the game.
2. Machine and repair choices: "Open the engine and fix it, with oily hands and no one to ask for help" and "Climb the tower and follow the broken gear by hand". Also Captain Rao: "The lamplighter once stayed up all night with me taking this engine apart. Oil to her elbows." and Ila saying "A mechanic, a mapmaker, a storyteller." He sees himself.
3. Pictures and the lighthouse. The cover art, the town pictures and the lit lighthouse with the first result of Builder at number 1 and "making and fixing things".

## 6. Story
Not really. He skips the intro at card 2. The lamplighter mystery has no stakes he can feel, and the keepers' quotes are too slow to read. Ila, the firefly Jyo and the lamplighter do not become people for him. He notices only two things: "mechanic" in Ila's line and the engine story. He would care if the lamplighter were someone who fixed things (a motor repairer or tower-clock mender) and he found a trail of tools she left. Spoken lines, a picture of each keeper, and a short "who is this?" would help. Skipping the story works well for him, but the game still pays for it later (the keepers' quotes appear after every town).

## 8. Lock puzzles: fun or exam-like?
- Numbers: fun for him, real, from the shop. Fair.
- Shapes and arrow patterns: fun, no reading. But shapes asks him to tell "turned around" from "flipped". With dyslexia (mirror and rotation confusion) this is exactly the thing that is harder for him. Flag it. (I picked one without working it out.)
- Words: exam-like and UNFAIR to him. "Map is to traveller as recipe is to ..." needs the analogy format and idea of a "helper tool for a person". "Which word means almost the same as 'gleam'?" is a plain English vocabulary test. Both are second-language and decoding tests. They also show "The lock holds this time" (a failure message). He would feel stupid and could quit here. Suggest: show the word locks as picture/odd-one-out, or make them optional with the same "Skip this lock" button, but he does not press skip because pressing it feels like admitting he cannot do it.
- No Hindi or read-aloud hint shown on the lock screens (see below).

## 10. Five suggestions, ranked
1. Add a Hindi mode (at least Hindi text for choices and results) and put a read-aloud speaker on every screen, including locks and the result. There is a speaker button ("Read this place and its choices aloud") at each town and a small waveform icon next to the story line, which is good. But there is none on lock screens (I saw only the sound toggle, no read-aloud), on the form, or on the result and field pages. A first choice of "हिंदी / English" on the cover would change this for Rohan.
2. Cut every choice line to under 8 words, and put the "downside" in a second smaller line so it can be skipped. Example: "Open the engine and fix it" (big), "oily hands, no one to ask" (small). Make the first words the verbs he knows.
3. Remove the second look at Orchard Village, or tell him at the start ("4 places, then a quick check of a few"). Make the counter exact ("Place 6 of 15") and drop "1 to 8 more", "up to 7 more".
4. Make the word locks fair or optional: show one line of picture meaning, add read-aloud, never say "The lock holds this time" (say "This key is not for this gate. Jyo shows another way." for every pick, wrong or right, as with the Numbers lock). Replace "Map is to traveller as recipe is to ..." and "gleam" with picture or number locks. Keep more of the money and measuring locks.
5. Shorten the first screens and the end. Make the consent text collapsible and read-aloud (it is about 500 words before the game starts). Show the top result and one "Try this week" line first, with India jobs and rupee pay; fold the 1,700-word field page, put "AI exposure" and US pay lower down.

## 11. Bugs and layout problems
- Tablet layout (820x1180): the game column is narrow (about 520px) and sits in the middle with blurred bars on both sides (shot_011, shot_019, shot_035). It does not use the screen, so text stays small on a tablet. Not a bug, but a large tablet could show larger text.
- "passers-by" in "Sell the fresh juice to passers-by on the village road" breaks over two lines at the hyphen (shot_011: "passers-" then "by on the village road"). Splitting a word is hard for a dyslexic reader.
- Counter wording: "Place 7 of 15 · 1 to 8 more", "Place 8 of 15 · up to 7 more", "Second look · 1 to 8 more". The numbers do not add up ("Second look · 2 to 9 more" after "1 to 8 more").
- The lock result text is the same container for right and wrong shapes ("The lock holds this time", "Click. The lock turns and the gate swings open.") so wrong/right is visible. Not a bug, but a risk.
- Town screens contain two paragraphs of text beneath the picture area in the page text ("You have two flasks of oil...") that are not visible in the screenshot (shot_011, shot_014). They are probably read by screen readers, so I could not tell what Rohan would actually see. Flagged as guess.
- Interaction: in a town I clicked two choices and "#light" worked, but when I tried to select a second choice, the picker needs two taps and no confirm shows which are chosen (the screenshot shows no ticks; I could not check this). Guess only.
