# Ishita (persona playtest, phone 390x844)

## 1. Persona
Ishita, 16, Class 11 PCM, Bengaluru. ADHD inattentive plus some anxiety. Likes astronomy, sketching, Canva for the school fest. Unsure about engineering. Drifts, misses one-time instructions, zones out by the 5th repeat, would rather quit than feel lost. Instruction screens were skimmed or skipped on purpose.

## 2. Did they finish? Quit point
Played to the end as a tester. In real life she would quit (or be on the edge of it) at **screen `stop` (Orchard Village, revisit "The lamp at Orchard Village is flickering, as if it wants a second look.")**, after **6 towns and 4 lock puzzles**, about **7 to 8 real minutes** in (my estimate: ~600 words read at ADHD skim speed plus 6 towns x ~25 s plus 4 locks x ~20 s, plus the long consent/welcome wall). Reason, in her words: "Wait, I did this one already. Is it broken? Did I mess up? How many more of these are there?" Top bar said "Place 1 of 15 · 7 to 14 more" at the very first town, so she already knew it was long. Second, weaker quit risk: the 2,300-word field page at the end.
She might have stayed one more screen for the lock puzzle with shapes (visual), and she definitely perked up at Festival Square and Observatory Hill, so the quit is "likely", not certain. I am guessing here; I cannot know.
Total minutes if she finished: roughly 12 to 14 real minutes to the finale (15 places + 6 locks + beats).

## 4. Top 5 moments that lost her
1. **Revisit of Orchard Village** (screen `stop`, "The lamp at Orchard Village is flickering, as if it wants a second look."). Looks like a bug or a repeat. For someone who loses the thread, "did I do this already?" is the worst feeling.
2. **The sign-up screen** ("What we keep, and who sees it ... We keep your name, the choices you make in the game and how long each place takes ..."). About 358 words before the game starts. She skims and feels like she is signing a contract.
3. **"Place 1 of 15 · 7 to 14 more"** at the first town. Tells her up front that it is long. The cover said "About 15 minutes" but the counter reads like a chore list.
4. **Town screens repeat the same shape** (4 cards, pick up to two, Light the lamp, Next) 15 times. By town 5 she is tapping the first thing that looks drawn.
5. **The 2,300-word field page** (Careers in this field with 7 cards, each "What these mean / How AI is used in this job today / Where no AI use has been seen yet" plus "Matches come from US job profiles"). Too much, too US, too grey. Also the vocab lock ("Which word means almost the same as 'ambiguous'?") felt like an exam.
Runners up: "Tap again to keep your oil and light" which times out after 2.6 s (see bugs).

## 5. Top 3 moments that worked
1. **The cover and the lighthouse finale art.** Real illustrations, warm light, firefly. She looks at them.
2. **Towns that match her interests**: Festival Square ("Create the welcome arch ... painting it as the lamps arrive") and Observatory Hill ("Chart the stars and wonder what is out there", "Paint the night sky"). She actually smiled.
3. **The shape-rotation and lamp-blink locks**: visual and quick, and the "Click. The lock turns and the gate swings open." response feels kind (no wrong-answer feedback, which suits her anxiety). Also the yellow "Try this week" box: concrete, small, actionable.

## 6. Story
She half cared about Ila (the "ferry lantern" girl, the three Ila beats) but did not remember why the lamplighter vanished. The torn note "A lamp burns best for hands that love their work" appeared once in the intro and was skipped. The town keeper lines ("Granny Pari ... about the lamplighter") are small grey text and she does not read them. The plot (find the lamplighter) never moves between towns, so there is no pull to see the next one. What would make her care: a visible mystery thread (a torn note piece to collect at each lamp, shown on the map), Ila reacting to HER choices by name, and the ending actually answering "where did she go?" The finale gives only a moral ("The road was never mine. Choose yours."), which is nice but is not an answer to the mystery she was asked to solve in the first line.

## 7. Questions
- Count: 15 towns (about 14 to 16 taps each incl. light and next) + 6 lock puzzles + class/board/stream. She felt "too many" from about town 5. The revisit of Orchard Village and the repeat of the shape and number lock types made it worse.
- Repetitive: every town is "pick from four jars", with the same oil-flask rule text (in the first-town screen: "You have two flasks of oil. Spend them on the two things you would most enjoy: both on one if you love it, or one on each. Pick what you really like, not a bit of everything. There are no right answers and nothing is marked."). On later towns the sentence is gone, which was fine for me but she would have missed it the first time anyway (see below).
- Confusing: the "Light the lamp · keep my oil" button when nothing is chosen. The meaning of "keep my oil" is not clear and the two-tap confirmation ("Tap again to keep your oil and light") disappears after 2.6 s.
- Confusing: the fields called "Creator / Thinker / Builder" vs the earlier words in the cards ("Run the press", "Teach children"). She never learns the six types while playing.
- Skipping instructions test: I skipped every instruction once. What happened:
  - The oil-flask rule is explained once in the intro and again on the first town. I never read either. I could still play (tap a jar, tap Light). Nothing broke, and I could light with one flask or none. So the instruction is not needed to play, but it is also not clear that the game WANTS one or two picks. The counter of flasks at the top is the only cue.
  - The lock puzzles' rule "Which one is the same shape, just turned around? A flipped copy does not count." sits above the picture; if she skips it she picks the mirror image, which is the trap. She cannot see that she got it "wrong" because every lock says "Click. The lock turns and the gate swings open." So skipping instructions costs nothing in the game but silently lowers the puzzle result.
  - Result page said all four puzzles "Strong" even though I guessed on the second Shapes lock. That looks like the puzzle score does not distinguish right from wrong, or this is just a rough band. (I cannot tell which; I am guessing.)
  - The consent sheet "Save your login code" is one-time info. I tapped "I have saved it" without saving. If she loses the tab, she has no way back. The login code reappears on the Welcome and result screens, so the risk is lower than the warning suggests.

## 8. Lock puzzles
Mixed. Shapes and lamp-blink were fun. Words ("Map is to traveller as recipe is to ...", "ambiguous") and number-series felt exam-like, as does the "A flask costs ₹40 ... 1 flask free for every 4 you buy" word sum. With her anxiety, they are tolerable only because the game says "Skip this lock" and never shows a wrong answer. But the "Skip this lock" button is below the fold on the Shapes screen on a phone ([offscreen: scroll]), so she would not find it.

## 9. Result
- Fit: Creator first, then Thinker, then Builder is very close to her (sketching, Canva, astronomy, engineering curiosity). "Design and creative arts" as the top field is right. "Science and research" third matches astronomy. Engineering is only a small "Also look at" chip, so her actual question ("is engineering right for me?") is not answered.
- Understanding: she understands the radar and the three cards. "Some of your top areas were tied, so the order of the top three is not fixed" is good and honest but is under a "Why?" link. She does not understand "Puzzle strengths: Strong x4" (all four said Strong, so it means nothing to her).
- Astronomy is invisible in the careers list (no astronomer, no space careers on the Design page; Science and research is third and was never opened by her).
- Would she show a parent? Probably yes, the radar and "Design and creative arts" cards, but the parent will see US pay in dollars, "Jobs 2035 -1.7%" and "Graphic Designer: AI exposure very high, in use". For an anxious kid, "very high AI exposure, in use now" and "-1.7% jobs" on exactly the career she likes can feel like "my dream is dying". The "Try this week" box is the best part for her.

## 10. Five suggestions, ranked
1. **Cut the walk to about 8 to 9 towns (3 per chapter) or make it clearly skippable ("finish now with what you have" button after town 6).** Fifteen is where the ADHD quit happens. If 15 are needed for measurement, adaptive stopping once the top three are stable would let her out sooner. Remove the "Place 1 of 15" text at the start; show "Chapter I of III" and a lamp-count that fills instead.
2. **Do not repeat Orchard Village (or any town) without saying so on screen.** If a revisit is a measurement check, say it in big words at the top: "Back at Orchard Village. Same place, new choices. Pick again, it is fine if it changes." Or change the scene art/story so it does not look identical.
3. **Make the story move.** Give each lamp a tiny story piece (a torn note fragment, a footprint, Ila's reaction to your choice) shown in 1 line and a picture, and pay it off at the end ("she went to ..."). Let the first screen after a lit lamp show the keeper's clue large, not small grey text.
4. **Shrink walls of text on the one-time screens.** Sign-up/privacy: 3 short lines with "Read more" folded; Welcome: one sentence plus the picture; first-town rules: 1 icon-led line shown above the jars ("2 flasks. Tap the ones you like.") and shown again as a small hint on any town where nothing is picked for 5 s. Make the "keep my oil" confirm stay for at least 5 s or just let a tap on "Light the lamp" with no oil work without a second tap.
5. **Make the result page short and personal first.** Put "Design and creative arts" with the "Try this week" box, 2 or 3 careers with India-relevant lines (UCEED, NID, NATA) and a one-line "Engineering: also fits your Builder side, here is a 1-minute look" before the 7 careers x 5 blocks of text. Fold the AI-exposure and US pay blocks behind a "More detail" tap. Mention her visible interests (Observatory Hill stars) by name in the result so it feels like it heard her.
