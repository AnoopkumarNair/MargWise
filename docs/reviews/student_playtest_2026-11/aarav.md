## 4. Top 5 moments that lost him
1. **Orchard Village shown a second time** (screen after Chapter II / Ila beat): "The lamp at Orchard Village is flickering, as if it wants a second look." Same four choices, reshuffled. He thinks it is a bug or that the game is padding.
2. **Lock 1, "Numbers"** (shot_019): "A flask costs ₹40. The shop gives 1 flask free for every 4 you buy. You need 10 flasks. How much do you pay?" It is a school word problem with four choices. Wrong answer gives only "The lock holds this time, so Jyo guides you round the side." No reward, no reason.
3. **Lock 2, "Words"**: "Map is to traveller as recipe is to ..." Exam-style analogy, and the number-series lock "2, 4, 3, 6, 5, 10, 9, ?" is the same problem.
4. **Sign-up screen** (358 words before he can start): "Margwise is a story game for students in Class 9 to 12..." plus "What we keep, and who sees it" in four paragraphs. He sees a wall of text and a form.
5. **Same loop 15 times**: "You have two flasks of oil. Spend them on the two things you would most enjoy: both on one if you love it, or one on each. Pick what you really like, not a bit of everything. There are no right answers and nothing is marked." That is 50 words of rule text on every town screen (shown in the first town; he stops reading it). Option text is long and ends on a downside: "Rope down a short stretch to test the rock, which is slow, tiring work".

## 7. Questions
- 15 towns (16 with the repeat) plus 8 locks felt like far too many. He would have wanted about 6 towns and 2 locks.
- Every town asks the same thing: pick two of four, press "Light the lamp · keep my oil". After town 3 the only new thing is the picture.
- Repeat of Orchard Village is confusing: it looks like an error.
- The wording of the oil rule is confusing: "both on one if you love it, or one on each... not a bit of everything". He taps two without reading.
- Options with the downside tacked on are long: "Check every harness against the safety list while others start climbing first", "Sort the medicine store so the right dose is quick to find, checking label after label". Not easy to skim on a phone.
- Class, board, stream screen at the end ("Two quick things first") is fine and quick.

## 8. Lock puzzles
Mostly exam-like. Numbers, Words and number series look like school tests and he skips or guesses. Shapes (block shapes like Minecraft) and Patterns (arrows) are interesting, but the shapes lock gave no feedback on why he was wrong and he missed it 3 times out of 3. "Skip this lock" is a lifesaver, but the first lock sits at the bottom, below the fold for the shapes puzzle (shot_034, the skip button is offscreen), which he would not scroll to. The text "any key you try teaches the lantern a little about you" makes the lock feel like a test even though the page says "Nothing is marked."

## 10. Five suggestions, ranked
1. **Cut the length hard.** Make towns 6 to 8 (not 15), with 2 locks total. Show "Lamp 3 of 6" with a visible fill bar. He would finish a 5-minute game.
2. **Never repeat a town.** Remove the Orchard second visit, or label it "Bonus round: quick re-check" with a reward. As it stands it looks like a bug.
3. **Replace exam locks with game-like ones.** Keep Patterns and Shapes (make them redstone/circuit puzzles: connect a wire, rotate a block). Drop word analogies and money word problems. Give a reason after a wrong shape answer, and a small win animation for every lock.
4. **Add visible progress and rewards.** A lamp counter with points, a streak ("3 lamps in a row"), a sound or spark each time a lamp lights, a collectable per town, and a "Builder 2 / Thinker 1" meter growing live so he sees his shape forming.
5. **Shorten every text screen to two lines.** Sign-up form: name, code, one tick; move "What we keep" behind a link. Town rule: one line "Pick 2 you'd enjoy" once on the first town only. Options of 6 to 8 words without the downside. Field page: show "Try this week" and three careers first, with the long details behind a tap, and show Indian examples, not US pay.
