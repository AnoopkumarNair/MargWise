## 4. Top 5 moments that lost him
1. Night Bazaar shown a second time as town 7. Same title, same four options, new order. Text: "The lamp at Night Bazaar is flickering, as if it wants a second look." Looks like the game is padding or broken.
2. The counter "Place 1 of 15 · 7 to 14 more" at the very first town. 15 towns plus 7 locks is the whole answer to "too many questions". He did the maths: "that's 22 screens of questions."
3. Lock puzzles that are exam questions: "Which word means almost the same as "ambiguous"?", "Which one is the same shape, just turned around? A flipped copy does not count.", "Two lamps blink together... 6 seconds and... 8 seconds". Framing says "any key you try teaches the lantern a little about you", but he feels tested.
4. Kid tone at the start: cover art of two kids with a firefly, "Best with a parent beside you", "Is a parent or guardian with you?", "a firefly who remembers every lamp". He decides it is "made for kids" in the first 30 seconds.
5. Nothing tells him what he gets until the end. Welcome says "show you the doors that open for you" and the Begin screen says "find the work that fits you". No example of an actual result, no "you will get a list of careers with pay and what to study".

(Also minor: every lamp lit needs an extra "Next" tap that shows only a quote about the lamplighter, "Last autumn she pressed apples with us from dawn, and sold the first jars herself."; he skims these every time.)

## 5. Top 3 moments that worked
1. "Skip the story" on the intro. He took it at once. Never forces the lore.
2. Night Bazaar / Printers' Quarter / Festival Square: commerce-flavoured towns. "Win over the passing buyers, knowing some will walk away" is exactly his life (creators, D2C). The "though..." tails make options feel real, not cartoon.
3. The result: radar shape, "Your top three: Persuader, Creator, Builder", then the "See which choices pointed here" panel (quotes his own picks back: "Festival Square: you picked "Manage the festival stalls..." (Persuader)"). It matches his picks, explains itself, and is the point where he would say "okay, this is legit." "Try this week: Run the budget for a school fest or a small stall" is concrete. Business and finance appeared as field #2 with the Commerce tag.

## 6. Story
He did not care about Ila or the lamplighter. He skipped the intro, so Ila appears out of nowhere after town 3 ("I am Ila. The lamplighter was my teacher. She left three days ago..."). The mystery never advances: every quote is "She once bargained for a whole bazaar's lamp oil and left every trader smiling." and the ending line "The road was never mine. Choose yours." does not answer anything. What would make him care: a stake he understands (a launch, a store, a channel, money), a quick hook in the first 20 seconds, Ila as a funny sarcastic friend or a rival instead of a sweet girl, and a reveal that the "lamplighter" clues add up (e.g. she was a founder who kept switching businesses). Honest guess: he would tolerate a thin story if it stayed out of the way, but not 6 beats of it.

## 7. Questions
- Count: 16 town screens plus 7 lock puzzles (plus class/board/stream) = about 24 decisions. Felt like too many from about town 5 onward. The real count and the promised 15 minutes do not match for a reader (est. 19 to 20 min for Kabir).
- Repetitive: Night Bazaar twice (s06 appears at town 3 and again at town 7, same four options re-ordered). Every town has the same layout: 4 options, pick up to 2, "Light the lamp · keep my oil". By town 8 he picks by position.
- Confusing: the instruction paragraph repeats under every town: "You have two flasks of oil. Spend them on the two things you would most enjoy: both on one if you love it, or one on each. Pick what you really like, not a bit of everything. There are no right answers and nothing is marked." (and it also stays visible on the result-of-lighting screen, after he has already spent the oil, which looks like a leftover). "Light the lamp · keep my oil" is unclear: does he keep oil or spend it? "Place 4 of 15 · 4 to 11 more" is a range he cannot read.
- Options with "though it may turn out to be junk" tails are good for realism but long on a phone.

## 8. Lock puzzles
Exam-like. 7 puzzles: numbers (flask price, LCM), words (analogy, synonym), shapes (rotation, which he skipped twice), patterns (arrows, number series). He enjoyed the flask price one only because it is shopping. The word ones read like Class 10 English paper. There is no right/wrong feedback ("Click. The lock turns and the gate swings open." even if wrong), so he asks "then why am I solving it?". "Skip this lock" is good. Result page shows "Puzzle strengths: Numbers Strong / Words Strong / Patterns Strong" although he skipped shape locks and guessed one; reads as flattery and makes him distrust it.

## 9. Result
Fit: mostly yes. Persuader / Creator / Builder, Business and finance (Commerce) and Performing arts (film, photography) match a video-editing D2C dreamer; "Builder" is the least him (random engine and stall-frame picks). He understood the shape and the "which choices pointed here" panel. Would he share it? He would screenshot the radar and top three for a friend/group ("Persuader lol") before he would show a parent. Turn-offs: "7 of the 9 careers below have very high AI exposure", "US pay $167k", "Jobs 2035 +6.9% in the US" (he is in Delhi and ignores US figures), and the Business field page is about 3,000 words: he scrolled and read three headings. The warning "A rough first draft... Most of your oil went to one kind of activity, so only your top area is a reliable signal" is honest but deflating after 15 towns. Missing for him: "founder / D2C brand", "content creator", "video editor" careers by name, and Indian pay or courses (it says "Matches come from US job profiles").

## 10. Five suggestions, ranked
1. Fix the repeated Night Bazaar (s06 twice, towns 3 and 7). Either remove the repeat or say clearly it is a "second look" at a place. Today it looks like a bug and is the single most likely quit moment.
2. Cut the number of towns and locks, or let him stop early. Add "See my result now" after about 8 towns (e.g. "Good enough? See your shape" and offer "Light more lamps for a sharper result"). Show total time left ("about 6 more minutes") instead of "Place 4 of 15 · 4 to 11 more".
3. Tell him the prize on the first screen: one line and a thumbnail of a result ("In 12 minutes you get: your top 3 interest areas, 3 fields, real careers, what to study after Class 11"). Replace "show you the doors that open for you".
4. Make the locks less like school: shorter (2 or 3 total), related to the story (price, margins, a shop sum, a "spot the pattern in these 5 reels"), show a tiny result after each ("Fast. Noted."), and drop the vocabulary and shape-rotation ones, or make Skip the default for all of them.
5. Grow-up the tone for 15-18 year-olds: drop "Best with a parent beside you" from the first screen and the "Is a parent or guardian with you?" screen (move the consent to a one-line footnote), cover art with older teens and a real city or startup street instead of two kids and a firefly, and a skip-by-default story. Also cut the extra "Next" tap after each lamp (merge the quote into the next town) and remove the spent-oil paragraph from the post-lamp screen.
