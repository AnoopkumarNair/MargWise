## 2. MW-03: the field-ranking skew, measured independently

Method (`scratchpad/rv4/t/mw03.mjs`): I loaded the built page and used `window.Margwise`. For all 120 ordered top-3 codes × 3 seeds (11, 22, 33) I ran `M.playJourney(M.levelsForCode(code), seed, M.JOURNEY_BANK, 0.8, same)`, then `M.journeyResult`. I then reproduced `fieldFits()` exactly: `content.fieldRef` percentiles, `fieldK=4`, `+0.002*raw`. These seeds and levels differ from the calibration set (`refcal.mjs` uses levels 1/.8/.6/.1 and seeds 100, 5000, 9000), so this is an out-of-sample check. Results are deterministic: 360 of 360 runs repeated exactly.

**First-place counts out of 360 (fair share is 22.5):**
arts 44, spo 43, des 38, psy 31, media 31, tec 25, health 23, voc 23, agr 21, mar 19, com 19, hos 12, eng 10, pub 9, law 7, **sci 5**. The range is 5 to 44 (about 9×).

**Top-3 appearances (fair share is 67.5):** arts 114, des 103, spo 95, media 93 … hos 67 … law 46, mar 43, tec 43.

- The three Artistic-heavy fields (design, arts, media) take 113 of 360 first places (31%), against 19% if all fields were equal. Sports is also high.
- Science (5), law (7) and public service (9) are the most under-represented. Hospitality sits about at its fair share.
- The PDF's direction is right. "Near-absence" fits the committed artefact (law 1 of 120, engineering 2 of 120) more than my run. "Hospitality concentration" does not reproduce.
- Without the percentile step, raw scores send first place to business 56, design 52 and health 44, and understanding people gets 1. Percentile normalisation moves the skew around; it does not remove it. Random tappers' top-3 (`refcal.json`) goes to design 164 and arts 162 against business 33.

**The ranking is fragile:**
- The gap between the top two fields is under 0.03 in **147 of 360 (41%)**.
- Only **152 of 360 (42%)** have a single field more than 0.05 ahead of the next.
- The median number of fields within 0.05 of the top is 2, and the 90th percentile is 4.
- The gap between field 3 and field 4 has a median of 0.039 and a 10th percentile of 0.0067. The puzzle nudge (±0.05) is bigger than most of these gaps.

**The profile shown upstream also overstates its order.** For consistent simulated players, the intended top-1 trait is recovered in 295 of 360 runs (82%) and the top-3 set in 283 of 360 (79%), but the exact ordered "Your top three" only in **162 of 360 (45%)**.

**Contributing causes found in the code:**
- Career counts per field run from 3 to 9. With `fieldK=4`, fields with 3 careers (law, tec, spo) average all of their careers, weak ones included. Business averages its best 4 of 9.
- The taxonomy has three overlapping Artistic fields.
- Some career placements are questionable. Forensic Science Technician (SOC 19-4092, a science technician) sits in Law and is shown as a "role to look at first" for law.

**Verdict:** the PDF's claim stands in substance: the ranking is skewed and not defensible as a strict order. It overstates "near-absence" and "hospitality".

**Defensible fix, in order:**
1. Make the RIASEC profile the main result, and say "top area" plus an unordered "also strong" set rather than an ordered top three.
2. Show fields as **bands, not a strict order**. Group every field within a tie margin of the leader as "Areas to explore". Set the margin from the seed-to-seed spread of the same profile, roughly ≥0.05 given the numbers above. Add a reason line per field, and an "explore beyond these" link that opens all fields.
3. Add a **frozen benchmark as a CI test** (`tests/bench_fields.json`): 120 codes × fixed seeds, recording each field's top-1 and top-3 rate. Fail if any field's top-3 rate falls outside, for example, 0.6× to 1.5× of its fair share, or if the benchmark changes without the fixture file being updated.
4. Fix the structure: give every field the same K (3, or one per field), check each career's placement (move Forensic Science Technician), and regenerate `fieldRef` inside the pipeline instead of out-of-band (see gap G3).
5. Keep the puzzle nudge off (MW-04).

## 4. Gaps the ChatGPT review missed (most severe first)

1. **High: the engine source is not in the repo.** The scorer exists only as a minified bundle inside `night-of-lamps.html` (Margwise IIFE with zod, about line 629). `ARCHITECTURE.md:123` says so. Nobody can review it, compare a second implementation written from a spec against it (the PDF's own proposal), or properly version it. Recover the source into `engine/` with a lockfile and build step. **L**
2. **High: there are no real tests.** `app/tests/*.mjs` print to the console and assert nothing. They also hard-code this session's temporary scratchpad path (`t25.mjs:6`, `refcal.mjs:5`), so they cannot run in CI or on another machine. **M**
3. **High: field calibration is out of band.** `fieldRef` comes from `refcal.mjs`, run by hand, and `extras.py:31` copies it in. `validate.py:44` only checks that every field has an entry. Any change to careers' `values` or `field` quietly makes the percentiles stale. Add a staleness check (hash of careers stored with the table) or regenerate it in the pipeline. **M**
4. **High: the change report hides ranking changes.** `diff.py` does not compare career `field`, `fieldRef`, `fieldK` or field `areas`/`name`/`blurb` (also listed under MW-10). **S**
5. **Medium: the privacy notice does not match the app.** The gate says "We never ask for your phone number", but "Save your lighthouse" asks for a 10-digit number (`svPhone`, line 1249; demo only, not sent). The page also loads Google Fonts and cdnjs GSAP with no SRI, so device IPs go to third parties the notice does not mention. Self-host both, or add SRI and update the notice. **S**
6. **Medium: the `SIGN_KEY` fallback also weakens IP hashing** (see MW-02). **S**
7. **Medium: content goes into innerHTML unescaped.** Field name, blurb, career title and line, and source `href` (lines 1307-1325) are inserted raw, and the CSP allows `'unsafe-inline'` scripts. Today the risk is low because content is trusted, but the weekly job scrapes O*NET text into `content.json`. Escape, or validate strings for `<` in `validate.py`. **S**
8. **Medium: the deployed page is not recorded.** `hosting/public/index.html` is gitignored and deploys are manual with wrangler (also under MW-11). **S**
9. **Medium: the admin API has no lockout, rate limit or audit log**, uses a single shared `ADMIN_KEY`, and lets anyone with the key delete testers or export CSVs without a record. **S**
10. **Low: invite use limits can be exceeded.** `join.js` checks `uses < max_uses` and later increments without a guard, so concurrent joins can go over the limit. Use `UPDATE … WHERE uses < max_uses RETURNING`. **S**
11. **Low: one validation rule does nothing.** `validate.py:134` is `… : pass`, so the PLFS group mapping is never checked (moot once `in` is removed). **S**
12. **Low: the "Your top three" order is shown as fixed** although only 45% of consistent simulated players get their intended order back (section 2). Show "top area + also strong". **S**
13. **Low: two copy bugs**: the dangling comma in the lean note and the "1 of the 3 … have" grammar (MW-09). **S**
